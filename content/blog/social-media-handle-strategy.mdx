## Handle Strategy Is Founder Work

Most startup advice focuses on product, fundraising, and hiring. Username strategy gets maybe a paragraph in a "brand checklist" blog post, usually near the bottom, after logo and colors.

This is a mistake. Your username is the permanent address of your brand on the internet. Unlike a domain name — which you can redirect, or a logo — which you can redesign, a username collision on a major platform can follow your brand for years.

This guide is what I wish existed when I was naming my first startup.

## Part 1: Choosing a Name You Can Claim

### The Availability Test

Before you fall in love with a name, run it through availability checks across:

- The `.com` domain (non-negotiable for most B2C and B2B startups)
- Twitter/X, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn (Tier 1 social)
- GitHub (if you're developer-facing)
- YouTube (even if you're not using it yet)
- The App Store and Google Play (check display names)

Handle Lookup makes this a 10-second check rather than a 30-tab browser session.

<Callout type="warn" title="The .com Trap">
  Don't evaluate the domain separately from social handles. A name with perfect social handle availability but a squatted .com domain is almost as bad as unavailable handles. Evaluate the whole package together.
</Callout>

### The Modifier Playbook

When your ideal name is partially taken, modifiers can preserve brand recognition while solving the availability problem. The best modifiers, ranked by perception:

1. **None** — Your exact name. Fight for it or change it.
2. **Prefix: "get"** — `getnotion`, `getlinear`, `getfigma` — common in SaaS, reads as a call to action
3. **Suffix: "hq"** — `notionhq`, `linearhq` — signals "official" without sounding desperate
4. **Suffix: "app"** — `notionapp` — clear, functional, widely understood
5. **Prefix: "use"** — `usenotion` — developer-adjacent, implies a tool
6. **Prefix: "the"** — `thenotion` — usually weaker, feels informal

Avoid: `_official`, `_real`, numbers appended to the end, and country codes (unless geo-specific). These signal inauthenticity.

### Name Testing Framework

Before committing, validate the name against three questions:

1. **Is it spellable from audio?** If you say "check us out at [name]" on a podcast, can listeners find you without asking for clarification?
2. **Does it survive autocomplete?** Type the first three letters of your name on each platform. Does your account surface, or does a competitor appear first?
3. **Can it get a .com within budget?** If the domain is taken but not actively used, what's the asking price? Factor it into your naming budget.

## Part 2: The Claim Sprint

Once you've chosen a name, speed matters. Your announcement, a press mention, or a viral tweet can trigger a wave of preemptive squatting within hours.

### Pre-Announcement Checklist

Complete this before any public announcement, blog post, or press release:

- [ ] Domain registered
- [ ] Twitter/X claimed
- [ ] Instagram claimed
- [ ] LinkedIn company page created
- [ ] GitHub org created (if developer product)
- [ ] TikTok claimed
- [ ] YouTube channel created
- [ ] Facebook page created (even if you won't use it)
- [ ] Threads claimed
- [ ] Bluesky claimed

For developer-specific products, also:
- [ ] npm org scoped (if relevant)
- [ ] PyPI project claimed (if relevant)
- [ ] Crates.io user claimed (if relevant)

<Callout type="info" title="The 24-Hour Window">
  After your first significant press hit, you have approximately 24 hours before systematic squatters notice and start registering variations. Do the claim sprint before launch, not after.
</Callout>

### Minimal Account Setup

Every claimed account should have, at minimum:

- **Profile picture**: Your logo. Even a placeholder is better than the default avatar.
- **Bio**: "[Your name] — [One-line description]. Find us at [URL]."
- **Website link**: Your homepage.
- **One post**: "We're just getting started. Follow for updates." This differentiates you from squatter accounts and helps with platform dormancy policies.

A five-minute setup per platform prevents misidentification and creates a defensible presence.

## Part 3: After the Launch

### Monitoring and Response

Post-launch, set up automated monitoring for:

1. **Exact match variations** — `yourname_`, `_yourname`, `yournameapp`, `getyourname`
2. **Common misspellings** — Users will find misspelled accounts and follow them thinking it's you
3. **Cross-platform gaps** — New platforms launch regularly; claim your handle as they emerge

When you find an impersonating account:

1. Screenshot evidence immediately
2. Report via the platform's impersonation flow (most have one)
3. If it has significant followers or is actively deceiving users, engage your trademark attorney

<Callout type="error" title="Impersonation Warning">
  Never contact a squatter to negotiate a handle purchase through a personal account — you may inadvertently reveal your trademark filing status or negotiating position. Route these through legal counsel.
</Callout>

### Platform-Specific Reclamation

Some platforms have specific processes for brand protection:

| Platform | Process | Timeline |
|----------|---------|----------|
| Twitter/X | Trademark infringement report | 2-6 weeks |
| Instagram | IP violation form | 1-4 weeks |
| GitHub | DMCA or username squatting report | Variable |
| TikTok | Trademark infringement form | 2-8 weeks |
| YouTube | Trademark complaint | 2-4 weeks |

Success rates are highest when you have a registered trademark and can demonstrate the account is misleading users, not just sharing your name.

## Part 4: The Long Game

### Building Handle Value

Your handle's value is a function of your brand's recognition. The bigger you get, the more valuable — and more contested — your handle becomes. This creates a paradox: you need the handle most when you're big, but it's hardest to reclaim then.

Strategies for protecting a growing brand:

- **Register your trademark early.** Even a pending trademark application significantly strengthens your position in platform disputes.
- **Post consistently.** Platform dormancy policies protect you — active accounts are almost never reclaimed without your consent.
- **Verify where possible.** Twitter/X Blue, Meta Verified, and YouTube verification badges signal legitimacy and make impersonation claims easier.

### When to Change Your Handle

Sometimes the right move is to change your handle, not fight for it. Consider changing if:

- You're paying more than $10K for a handle that's already your third choice
- The current holder's audience is significantly different from yours (you'd inherit confusing search results)
- The conflict is creating customer support overhead that outweighs the value of the handle

Some of the most recognizable brands today succeeded despite early handle mismatches. Don't let handle obsession paralyze a launch.

## Summary

Username strategy isn't glamorous, but it's foundational. The framework:

1. **Test availability before naming** — Handle Lookup makes this fast
2. **Sprint to claim before launch** — 24 hours matters
3. **Set up minimal presence everywhere** — Prevents squatting, costs nothing
4. **Monitor continuously** — Automation beats manual checking
5. **Protect legally** — Register your trademark before you need it

Your handle is your brand's address on the internet. Treat it like infrastructure, and it'll take care of itself. Treat it as an afterthought, and it'll be a recurring headache.
